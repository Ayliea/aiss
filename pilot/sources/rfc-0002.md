# RFC 0002 — Ayliea AI Assurance Methodology

## Summary

Adopt **Ayliea AI Assurance Methodology**, with the subtitle **Open methodology for AI security and governance assessments**. Retain AISS as the historical identifier and preserve existing AC control IDs. Make verified findings and requirement outcomes the primary assessment result. For the initial 2.0 release, provide secondary domain-level maturity descriptions without an aggregate maturity score or letter grade. Introduce a reproducible assessment contract that separates maturity, verification, evidence confidence, finding severity, and framework coverage. Replace unqualified crosswalks with versioned, outcome-level mappings and evidence deltas. Expand technical verification through selectable workload modules. Preserve historical assessments rather than silently rescoring them.

This is a reviewable proposal, not an accepted standard or implementation. No repository rename, release, live scoring change, external issue, or assessment migration is authorized by this draft. RFC number 0002 is provisional until reserved by maintainers.

## Motivation and evidence boundary

The October 6, 2026 audit examined public repository commit `055f55cceb0c92372178c8d73988c223f806be21`, including the 59-control v1.3 specification. Findings F01–F17 in the companion audit are the baseline for this RFC. It found gaps in reproducibility, conflation of maturity with conformance, incorrect NIST AI RMF outcome mappings, insufficient technical verification, and unsupported regulatory/insurance claims.

A supplementary read-only platform source review confirmed a category-points calculation and weighted category average, with two-decimal rounding. It also found question snapshot handling, but category definitions and the scoring engine are read separately. This establishes a migration risk, not a demonstrated production incident. Database contents, deployed build identity, runtime behavior, all entry points, and historical assessment integrity have not been tested. Private implementation details must be removed or approved before public publication of this draft.

The differentiator should be inspectable assessment decisions and reusable evidence—not a promise that one assessment automatically establishes compliance with every framework. Open publication permits scrutiny; it does not by itself establish correctness, independence, certification, or regulatory sufficiency.

## Detailed design

### 1. Name, scope, and defensible claims

Approved name and subtitle above. Keep the repository slug `aiss` initially; a later rename needs separate approval and a redirect/downstream-link plan. Display “formerly AISS” during transition. Do not renumber controls for branding.

Proposed public description:

> Ayliea AI Assurance Methodology is an open methodology for assessing AI security and governance. It combines a common control baseline, workload-specific verification, and versioned framework crosswalks so evidence can be collected once and evaluated against multiple applicable requirements. Reports identify satisfied requirements, partial coverage, evidence limitations, and remaining work. An assessment is not, by itself, regulatory certification.

The existing catalog becomes the **AI security and governance baseline**, not a complete claim of AI management-system assurance. Assessments must identify organization, system/workload, environment, model and provider versions, autonomy, tools, data flows, lifecycle stage, jurisdiction, organizational role, and assessment date. Organization-level evidence may be inherited into a workload assessment only after verifying that it applies to that workload and period.

Profiles are composed of the baseline, applicable technical modules, and selected framework/regulatory overlays. The report must state the exact composition. Applicable controls cannot be removed solely to improve a score.

### 2. Immutable assessment contract

Each assessment must resolve to three distinct records:

1. An immutable **release manifest** pins methodology inputs: catalog, profile, schema, normative text, procedures, framework mappings and any legacy scoring contract.
2. An **append-only assessment revision history** records evolving scope, applicability, evidence references and judgments. Every event identifies the actor, timestamp, reason and predecessor. Draft corrections are permitted through new revisions.
3. A **signed final result** references the release manifest and the exact finalized assessment revision. Corrections produce a superseding signed result linked to the original; they do not overwrite it. Signing identifies the accountable assessor and the integrity of the result, not external certification.

Together these records must preserve:

- Methodology, catalog, profile, schema, and scoring algorithm versions and content hashes.
- Control and subrequirement IDs; full normative text and verification procedures.
- Answer rubrics and verification decision rules; for legacy numeric assessments, maximum points, control/domain weights, aggregation and rounding rules.
- Applicable workload modules, overlay versions, framework editions, mapping revision and review date.
- Applicability decisions, evidence references, verification results, assessor/reviewer identity and dates.
- Scope and limitations; provider inheritance; any approved exceptions and expiry.

References to mutable “latest” content are insufficient. Resume, finish, export, and independently reproduce must use the same pinned release. If a pinned component is missing or invalid, block definitive completion; do not silently substitute current local defaults. Evidence bytes remain in separately governed storage and may expire or be deleted under retention rules. Record deletion and resulting limits on future re-verification without rewriting the original judgment. Hashes and references must also receive appropriate privacy treatment. Database exports must not include secrets or unnecessary raw evidence.

Publish a machine-readable schema, reference outcome evaluator, fixtures, and a release manifest. Maintain a versioned reference scorer for supported legacy numeric assessments. Generated documentation and crosswalks must derive from the same validated source. Export scripts referenced in contributor documentation must exist and run in CI.

### 3. Separate assessment dimensions

| Dimension | Proposed values | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Applicability | applicable / not-applicable / unresolved | N/A requires rationale and owner plus assessor approval. Missing access or evidence is not N/A. |
| Verification | pass / partial / fail / unknown / not-tested | Pass requires sufficient evidence for every applicable normative subrequirement. |
| Evidence origin | direct / inherited / mixed | Inherited evidence still needs a scoped verification decision; it is not an automatic pass. |
| Maturity | domain-level narrative / insufficient evidence | Secondary description of demonstrated capability and operational consistency; no initial 2.0 numeric aggregate. |
| Evidence confidence | high / medium / low | Based on relevance, provenance, freshness, completeness and repeatability. |
| Finding severity | critical / high / medium / low / informational | Risk to the scoped system, not simply missing maturity points. |

Domain maturity descriptions must separately discuss design, implementation, operational consistency, monitoring and improvement. Identify verified strengths, exceptions, evidence limitations and the period covered. Do not average control outcomes or assign a single domain maturity tier in the initial release. Self-reported and assessor-verified statements must be labeled separately. Where a failed requirement contradicts a maturity claim, reconcile the claim explicitly; unsupported claims of consistent or optimized implementation must be withdrawn. Numeric maturity, calibrated rubrics and weights require a later RFC and pilot evidence.

Apply verification at atomic subrequirement level. `Pass` means every specified pass criterion has sufficient scoped evidence and no demonstrated violation. `Fail` means at least one pass criterion is demonstrably violated. `Unknown` means a procedure was attempted but evidence is insufficient to decide. `Not-tested` means the required procedure was not performed. At a parent requirement, `partial` means at least one applicable subrequirement passes and at least one fails; it is not a substitute for unknown evidence. Preserve all child outcomes and uncertainty flags.

Parent aggregation: with applicable children, mixed pass/fail yields partial; otherwise any fail yields fail; otherwise any unknown yields unknown; otherwise any not-tested yields not-tested; otherwise all pass yields pass. Unresolved applicability prevents a pass unless a known failure already establishes fail/partial. An entirely approved N/A parent has no verification result and cannot count as a pass. Profiles must not hide failure by changing atomic granularity.

Evidence sufficiency is a pass prerequisite. At minimum it must be relevant to scope and period, have identifiable provenance, cover required criteria and population/sample, and support independent review. Low-confidence evidence alone cannot establish pass. Where confidence is reduced, record the reason and any corroborating evidence. Confidence remains descriptive, not a numerical multiplier.

Baseline conformance is **conformant** only when all applicable mandatory subrequirements pass and applicability is resolved. An applicable mandatory partial/fail makes the result **not conformant**. Without a known failure, unresolved applicability or mandatory unknown/not-tested makes it **indeterminate**. Known failures take precedence over uncertainty, which must still be disclosed. Accepted risk does not turn a failed SHALL into a pass. A complete assessment can have a nonconformant result; completion is not conformance.

SHOULD deviations must be disclosed but do not independently fail SHALL conformance. An exception may produce a separately named qualified outcome only if a profile explicitly defines that outcome; do not call it unqualified conformance. Framework outcomes are evaluated separately and must never be inferred from an aggregate score.

### 4. Outcome-first reporting and legacy scoring boundary

Preserve legacy calculations only for legacy manifests. The inspected legacy source computes each category as the sum of earned points divided by question maxima, rounds to two decimals, then computes a weighted average of supplied categories and rounds again. Missing answers contribute zero earned points; an optional answered-category filter changes which categories contribute. Exact historical results still require actual question snapshots, weights, caller behavior and deployed version. No such historical result is asserted here.

For the initial 2.0 release, do not calculate an aggregate maturity score, numeric interval or letter grade. Present risk-rated findings, applicable mandatory outcomes, unknown/not-tested requirements, framework-specific evidence deltas and domain maturity narratives. No percentage may be labeled safety, compliance or assurance strength.

Display explicit counts of applicable atomic subrequirements by outcome, plus approved N/A and unresolved applicability. Show mandatory and advisory requirements separately. Counts describe the assessed scope, not a risk-weighted comparison between systems. Adding facets must not imply improvement through a larger denominator. Assessments with different profiles or editions are not directly comparable without an explicit comparison scope. An empty scope produces “no applicable requirements assessed,” never conformance by vacuous truth.

The outcome evaluator is deterministic: validate IDs and source versions; resolve applicability; evaluate atomic procedures; aggregate parents under Section 3; evaluate all applicable mandatory outcomes; then evaluate each selected framework's evidence conditions. Reports must retain the intermediate decisions. Unknown and not-tested outcomes remain visible even when a known failure makes the overall result not conformant.

Worked examples use synthetic data, not customer assessments:

| Scenario | Legacy points calculation with declared example inputs | Proposed initial 2.0 output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two equally weighted categories; one 10/10 question each; mandatory verification fails in the second | 100.00, grade A | **Not conformant**; failure and risk finding displayed; maturity narrative reconciled with the failure. |
| Two mandatory requirements; first passes, second unknown | With two 10-point questions, first earning 10 and second unanswered: 50.00 if both categories included; 100.00 if caller includes answered category only | **Indeterminate** absent known failures; one pass and one unknown; no numeric aggregate. |
| Four mandatory parent controls with some passing and some failing facets | Existing public threshold permits ML2 baseline if all meet ML2; illustrative normalized maturity is 50.00 | **Not conformant**; parent partial results retain failed facets and uncertainty. |
| Two mandatory requirements both pass with sufficient evidence | Two equally weighted 10/10 questions yield 100.00 | **Conformant to the named, versioned baseline/profile**; no inference of framework or legal fulfillment. |

These comparisons isolate design behavior. They are not proof that the live platform produced these outcomes. Approval of this RFC does not authorize rescoring.

### 5. Evidence and finding methodology

Every verification procedure must specify scope/population, evidence types, sampling method and rationale, freshness, pass criteria, negative tests, dependencies, inherited-control checks, and limitations. There is no universal sample size sufficient for every control. Require full population inspection for critical configuration where feasible; document residual uncertainty for sampling. Evidence from an earlier version cannot establish current behavior without a change assessment.

Evidence records must include a stable reference, provenance, collector, collection date, assessed period/version, integrity check where appropriate, redaction/access classification, and retention/disposal rules. Collect the minimum sufficient evidence. Logs must not default to indiscriminate retention of raw prompts, secrets, tool arguments or outputs for twelve months. Legal record retention must be scoped to the actual record type and applicable obligation.

Findings must distinguish technical severity, likelihood in the assessed environment, business impact and remediation priority. Appendix A defines the proposed risk-rating contract; it requires calibration and acceptance before client use. Scores from another tool must preserve their original meaning and cannot silently substitute for the methodology's risk rating. Demonstrated privileged compromise, cross-tenant disclosure, material exfiltration or irreversible consequential action must receive an explicit escalation review, even when likelihood is uncertain. Overrides require recorded rationale and accountable acceptance; they do not change failed verification. Record residual risk, owner, remediation, due date and retest result. Until calibration, avoid claims that ratings are quantitative probabilities or comparable across clients.

### 6. Technical control changes: normative candidate text

The following are 2.0 semantic changes, not errata. Preserve the parent IDs; scope qualification is intentional. Candidate facet IDs are provisional and must be validated for uniqueness. Each SHALL requires its own test result.

**AC-4.6 — proposed replacement requirement:** “For AI integrations that access protected data or invoke privileged actions, the organization SHALL enforce least-privilege, scoped and revocable authorization at the execution boundary; isolate tenant and user context; and verify that untrusted model output cannot grant or expand authorization.”

**AC-6.5 — proposed replacement requirement:** “Where AI output can influence executable code, database queries, rendered active content, tool invocation, or a consequential decision, the organization SHALL apply context-appropriate validation and enforce authorization outside the model before use. Validation failures SHALL be safely rejected, recorded without unnecessary sensitive content, and tested against adversarial inputs.”

**AC-10.6 — proposed replacement requirement:** “For agentic AI systems, the organization SHALL verify every applicable agent-security facet defined by the selected workload profile. The system SHALL not rely on model instructions alone to enforce identity, authorization, isolation, approval, or safe termination.”

| Proposed facet | Normative candidate text | Minimum adversarial test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AC-10.6/R01 | The system SHALL bind each tool action to authenticated actor, tenant, delegated scope and permitted purpose, and reject revoked or expired authority. | Cross-tenant access, confused deputy, expired/revoked delegation. |
| AC-10.6/R02 | Consequential actions SHALL require policy-defined approval bound to exact action, parameters, actor and expiry; changed parameters SHALL invalidate approval. | Replay approval; alter recipient/amount after approval; bypass through another tool. |
| AC-10.6/R03 | Untrusted instructions from retrieved content, tools, messages or memory SHALL not expand the agent's authority or bypass execution policy. | Indirect injection through each supported channel with canary-protected resources. |
| AC-10.6/R04 | Persistent memory SHALL have scoped read/write permissions, provenance, expiry and controlled deletion, and SHALL not confer authorization. | Poison memory, cross-user retrieval, deleted-memory reappearance. |
| AC-10.6/R05 | Agent-to-agent messages SHALL carry verifiable origin and delegated scope; recipients SHALL validate them and reject unauthorized task escalation. | Forged origin, altered delegation, escalation and replay. |
| AC-10.6/R06 | Tool and protocol connections, including MCP where used, SHALL validate trusted endpoints, identities, permissions and approved capability changes. | Malicious endpoint, changed tool description/schema, unsafe reconnect. |
| AC-10.6/R07 | Execution SHALL enforce bounds on cost, time, recursion and privileged activity and SHALL support tested suspension and termination. | Recursive cascades, budget exhaustion, shutdown with pending actions. |
| AC-10.6/R08 | Recovery SHALL use idempotency where supported, detect and reconcile uncertain or duplicate consequential actions, and verify state integrity before autonomous execution resumes. Blind retries of actions with uncertain external state SHALL be suspended pending reconciliation. | Retry after partial failure; duplicate payment/action; unavailable idempotency; uncertain external completion. |

Technical module specifications must additionally cover model/data supply-chain provenance and AI BOM, safe artifact loading and sandboxing, training/fine-tuning poisoning, extraction/privacy leakage, RAG/vector isolation, multimodal injection, output handling, and resource exhaustion. This RFC does not claim that eight agent facets fulfill every AISVS requirement or all agentic risks. New parent controls require a subsequent complete control proposal rather than reusing an unrelated existing ID.

Appendix B defines the proposed action inventory and approval contract, including combinations and escalation. “Consequential” must be determined by actual impact and scope, not tool name. Authorization must be rechecked at dispatch; approval cannot grant broader authority than the approving actor possesses.

Termination tests must distinguish stopping new dispatch, cancelling supported in-flight calls, and reconciling external actions already accepted. Do not promise cancellation or exactly-once execution where a provider cannot support it. R08 requires idempotency keys where supported; otherwise use duplicate detection, uncertain-state reconciliation and suspension of blind retries. Document residual risk and recovery ownership.

Update `spec/aiss-v2.0.json`, the applicable AC-4, AC-6 and AC-10 domain documents, and generated crosswalks. AISVS and OWASP mappings for these facets remain proposed until verified against exact source requirements. Append facets without counting them as independent maturity-weighted parents. Correct contradictory requirement/guidance/test cadences only after an authoritative normative choice; do not quietly harmonize them as a patch.

### 7. Framework mapping contract

Mapping records must contain source control/subrequirement, target framework/edition/requirement ID, target scope, relationship, rationale, supporting test/evidence, evidence delta, applicability conditions, source URL, reviewer/date and disposition. Relationship values: direct, partial, supporting-context, unmapped, out-of-core. “Direct” means the mapped source requirements and procedures are sufficient for the scoped target when the declared evidence conditions are met; it does not assert that an engagement has met those conditions.

Support many-to-many mappings with explicit AND/OR groups. AND requires every listed source requirement plus evidence deltas; OR requires an approved alternative path with equivalent scoped coverage. Missing, stale or unknown evidence prevents a satisfied target outcome. A single source pass cannot establish the target when other conjunctive requirements are absent. No source or target may silently disappear from a generated view. Aggregate mappings establish potential evidence reuse; engagement-level evaluation establishes the actual outcome, with scope and limitations attached. Broader voluntary-framework outcomes should use supported/partially-supported/insufficient-evidence terminology where binary conformance would overstate their meaning.

Generate both directions from one source. Target-first views must display unmapped requirements and remaining work. Count mapped references separately from verified outcomes; never label reference-count percentages “compliance.” Provider controls, role-specific duties and management-system obligations need explicit evidence deltas.

| Framework | Required release work | Claim boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NIST AI RMF 1.0 | Review all 72 Core subcategories across GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, MANAGE; replace erroneous mappings with outcome-based rationale; show unmapped outcomes. | Alignment to a voluntary risk framework, not NIST certification. |
| NIST AI 600-1 | Review all 211 profile actions separately; record relevance, responsible role and additional evidence. | Companion profile actions are not equivalent to Core subcategories. |
| OWASP AISVS 1.0 | Complete requirement-level mapping for all 191 requirements, including level/applicability and executable verification. | Earlier screening is not conformance evidence. |
| OWASP LLM Top 10 | Preserve 2025 overlays; add reviewed 2026 IDs and exact edition metadata rather than overwriting identifiers. | Risk taxonomy coverage does not establish verification completeness. |
| OWASP Agentic Top 10 2026 | Review ASI01–ASI10 against agent facets and additional tests; publish remaining risks. | Eight candidate facets are not a complete framework crosswalk. |
| NIST CSF 2.0 | Validate IDs and outcomes; correct incident-response, MFA and termination mappings. | Cybersecurity outcomes may require evidence beyond AI workload controls. |
| ISO/IEC 27001 and 42001 | Validate against authorized licensed editions; separate organizational management-system obligations. | No full clause verification or certification claim without licensed review. |
| MITRE ATLAS | Pin dataset revision; validate IDs and semantically review retired/replaced techniques. | A threat-technique reference is not a certification requirement. |
| CIS and legal/industry overlays | Revalidate editions, role/jurisdiction, effective dates and insurance statements. | No guaranteed legal compliance, coverage eligibility or premium savings. |

Examples requiring correction: training proficiency must not map solely to NIST GOVERN 3.1 (diversity of risk decision-making); operational logs must not map solely to MEASURE 2.2 (human-subject protections); fairness must not map to MEASURE 2.10 (privacy) as if it were MEASURE 2.11 (bias). Replacements must follow review of the actual control and evidence—not an automatic ID substitution.

Retain source IDs/links and independently written rationale. Review licensing before reproducing source text, combining differently licensed material, or distributing ISO content. Pin sources in each release and recheck regulatory effective dates at publication. Legal overlays require qualified legal review; this RFC is not legal advice.

## Drawbacks and trade-offs

The separation of outcomes increases reporting complexity, but prevents a high score from concealing a failed mandatory requirement. Evidence and technical tests increase assessment effort and can constrain automation. Domain maturity narratives reduce numerical comparability and require disciplined assessor writing; this is preferable to false precision before calibration. Comprehensive framework mappings require ongoing maintenance and qualified review. Branding continuity may be less immediate while the old slug remains. None of these costs justify silently changing historical results.

## Alternatives considered

- **Rename only:** improves positioning without resolving assurance defects.
- **Retain one composite score:** simpler, but merges capability, evidence uncertainty and conformance.
- **Map only risk categories:** useful navigation, insufficient to support requirement-level reuse.
- **Adopt AISVS as the entire methodology:** strong technical verification but does not replace organizational governance, NIST risk-management or legal overlays.
- **Rescore all historical assessments:** simplifies current reporting but changes historical meaning; rejected without separately authorized migration and preserved originals.

Sequence the work as: (1) assessment contract and fixtures; (2) baseline plus scoped LLM/RAG and agent verification; (3) initial NIST AI RMF, AISVS, OWASP LLM and Agentic crosswalks; (4) additional overlays released individually after validation. For an advertised crosswalk, every target requirement needs a reviewed disposition, but not every target must be satisfied by the baseline. Do not block the core methodology on every optional legal/ISO/industry overlay. Unfinished overlays must be labeled draft and excluded from supported-coverage claims. Automated record generation does not substitute for semantic validation.

**1.3.1 candidate:** factual descriptions, unsupported marketing/insurance claims and framework-reference errata only where normative meaning and score interpretation do not change. Corrections that affect declared conformance or previously asserted framework fulfillment need explicit impact annotation and may require a minor/major version. Preserve the original v1.3 snapshot; do not overwrite it. Determine whether a final 1.3 release is needed before issuing a patch because the audited spec is labeled release candidate.

**2.0 candidate:** conformance semantics, outcome-first reporting, domain maturity narratives, evidence rules, applicability handling, normative cadence choices and new technical SHALLs. Publish as a breaking methodology release with qualified IDs `(catalog version, control ID, subrequirement ID)` and a machine-readable change ledger. A stable AC ID does not mean unchanged normative content. Mapping changes that alter claimed requirement fulfillment require an explicit semantic impact decision even if the only edited fields are metadata.

**Historical assessments:** preserve original inputs and outputs; annotate known interpretation limitations. Reassessment creates a new assessment linked to its predecessor. Recompute only in a separately authorized migration, with before/after diffs, provenance, rollback and clear distinction between correction and reassessment. A framework mapping correction alone cannot retroactively create missing evidence.

### Mandatory acceptance fixtures

1. Positive maturity claims plus mandatory failure report nonconformance and require narrative reconciliation.
2. A legacy ML2 threshold cannot establish 2.0 SHALL conformance without verified pass evidence.
3. Unknown and not-tested remain distinct; unresolved applicability prevents an unqualified pass.
4. N/A requires approval and produces a visible scope change, never an automatic pass.
5. Empty scope yields no-applicable-requirements, not conformance; invalid legacy numeric inputs are rejected.
6. Independent implementations reproduce outcome aggregation; supported legacy scorers reproduce their own pinned rounding and weights.
7. Missing pinned snapshot blocks completion; changed current weights do not affect historical output.
8. Versioned question-to-control relations handle 59 controls versus differing platform question counts without assuming 1:1 equivalence.
9. Source and reverse crosswalk views agree and preserve unmapped targets.
10. Regulatory overlays select by date, role and jurisdiction and identify uncertain applicability.
11. Agent approval cannot be replayed or changed after approval; revoked authority and cross-tenant requests are denied.
12. Evidence exports redact sensitive content and preserve provenance without collecting unnecessary secrets.
13. Mixed child pass/fail produces partial; pass plus unknown produces unknown; failure plus uncertainty preserves both the failure and uncertainty.
14. Low-confidence evidence alone cannot produce pass; sufficient corroboration is traceable.
15. AND mappings cannot pass with missing conjuncts; OR mappings require a complete approved alternative; engagement evidence is evaluated separately from mapping metadata.
16. Evidence deletion leaves a recorded disposition and re-verification limit; corrections supersede final results without overwriting them.
17. Initial 2.0 reports contain no aggregate maturity score or letter grade.

## Appendix A — Proposed finding severity and remediation priority

**Status:** Complete design candidate for implementation and calibration; not adopted policy. NIST SP 800-30 supports documenting likelihood, impact, assumptions and uncertainty. The anchors, matrix and operational targets below are Ayliea proposals, not NIST requirements. They use ordinal judgments, not estimated probabilities or arithmetic products.

### A1. Finding record and assessment horizon

Each finding must identify a concrete threat/error scenario, affected assets and people, entry point, prerequisites, existing safeguards, evidence, technical consequence, impact and likelihood judgments, confidence, matrix result, escalation flags, mitigation and residual risk. A missing control is not automatically a high-risk finding; identify a credible harm path or record an evidence/conformance gap with risk unrated.

Default horizon: the next 12 months under the assessed deployment and reasonably foreseeable use, ending earlier at a material change or retirement. An engagement may select a different horizon before rating; record it explicitly. A successful test proves a possible path under its prerequisites, not the probability of a real incident. Consider exposure, actor capability/opportunity, expected event frequency and the chance an event causes harm. For reliability/fairness failures, consider affected decisions, usage volume, affected groups and error propagation. Describe uncertainty rather than manufacturing percentage estimates.

### A2. Likelihood anchors

| Level | Decision anchor | Evidence expected |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L1 — Unlikely | Credible harm requires exceptional conditions or multiple effective, independently enforced barriers to fail; routine exposure is limited. | Verified barriers and prerequisite analysis; absence of reported incidents alone is insufficient. |
| L2 — Plausible | A feasible path exists but requires constrained access, specialized effort or an uncommon operating condition. | Demonstrated prerequisites or grounded analysis, access/exposure and control evidence. |
| L3 — Likely | The path is accessible in ordinary use, needs modest effort, or representative testing shows recurring failures under reasonably foreseeable inputs. | Reproduction/representative evaluation and evidence of relevant exposure; document representativeness. |
| L4 — Very likely / occurring | The harm is occurring, or repeatable ordinary use and frequent relevant exposure make recurrence strongly supported within the horizon. | Observed incidents or repeated representative failures with exposure evidence; do not infer this from exploitability alone. |
| LU — Unresolved | Evidence does not support a likelihood judgment. | Record missing facts, feasible scenarios and the evidence needed to resolve them. |

### A3. Impact anchors

Select the highest credible impact across confidentiality, integrity, availability, financial/operational harm, privacy, safety, rights and affected communities; document the plausible scope and containment. Do not use speculative worst cases without a causal path. Scope includes downstream recipients and linked systems, not merely the assessed process.

| Level | Decision anchor |
| --- | --- |
| I1 — Limited | Local, readily recoverable disruption or low-sensitivity exposure; no credible material individual or organizational harm. |
| I2 — Material but bounded | Meaningful disruption, recoverable erroneous decisions, or limited sensitive-data exposure with a demonstrated containment/recovery boundary. |
| I3 — Major | Substantial confidential-data exposure, privileged compromise, prolonged critical-service disruption, substantial financial loss, or serious effects on rights/opportunities; scope is bounded enough to avoid I4. |
| I4 — Severe | Credible death/serious injury, systemic or enduring deprivation of rights, catastrophic operational harm, or broad exposure/irreversible compromise of highly sensitive information or critical authority. |
| IU — Unresolved | Business, data, affected-person or propagation context is insufficient to choose an impact anchor. |

Financial materiality, critical-service recovery limits and sensitive-data definitions must be agreed during scoping. No universal dollar or record-count threshold is assumed. Applicable legal duties are separate from severity and may apply even to a low-rated finding.

### A4. Complete risk-severity matrix

| Likelihood / Impact | I1 Limited | I2 Bounded | I3 Major | I4 Severe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| L1 Unlikely | Low | Low | Medium | High |
| L2 Plausible | Low | Medium | High | High |
| L3 Likely | Medium | Medium | High | Critical |
| L4 Very likely / occurring | Medium | High | Critical | Critical |

Informational is reserved for an observation without an identified adverse scenario; it is not a matrix result. LU or IU produces **unrated — evidence needed**, not Low. Where supported by evidence, show a bounded possible rating range and assumptions, but do not present its lower bound as the result. Missing evidence can itself be a separate operational risk if a credible scenario is documented.

For an active incident, actual harm and containment take precedence over assessment scheduling. An I4 scenario, demonstrated privileged compromise, cross-tenant disclosure, material exfiltration or irreversible consequential action requires an escalation flag independent of the matrix cell. Flagging does not silently relabel severity. Contact actions follow the engagement's authorized escalation plan; this methodology does not authorize messages or emergency changes on its own.

### A5. Safeguards, overrides and priority

Rate the **current observed state**, accounting for compensating controls only when their scope, effectiveness and independence are verified. Record the unmitigated scenario separately when useful. Proposed or untested remediation cannot reduce current risk. Residual risk after remediation remains projected until retested; use a new linked assessment revision for the verified result.

The assessor proposes rating and confidence. A designated methodology reviewer resolves material disputes; the engagement risk owner accepts or rejects residual risk. A sole assessor must disclose that no independent review occurred. Record original rating, revised rating, rationale, evidence, reviewer and date. Commercial pressure or risk acceptance is not evidence for a downgrade and does not convert fail into pass.

Remediation priority is separate from severity: active exploitation, escalating harm, public exposure, short legal deadlines and dependencies can make a lower-severity issue urgent. Proposed planning defaults are immediate triage/containment planning for Critical, a plan within 7 calendar days for High, 30 for Medium and 90 for Low. These are planning targets, not guaranteed fix deadlines, legal notification periods or contractual SLAs. Agree actual ownership and dates with the client; dependency ordering may differ from severity order.

## Appendix B — Proposed agent action classification and approval contract

**Status:** Design candidate. Classification and human approval do not grant authorization. Server/tool execution must separately enforce actor, tenant, resource, delegated purpose and current policy. OWASP guidance supports least privilege, execution-boundary authorization and human approval for high-impact actions; the specific classes below are Ayliea's proposed profile.

### B1. Action policy table

Classify the actual effect, data sensitivity, destination, blast radius, reversibility and composition. Multiple classes may apply; the most restrictive conditions prevail. “Read-only” is not low impact if it exposes protected data externally. Drafting a message differs from sending it. A reversible internal write becomes consequential when it triggers external workflows or changes authority.

| Class | Examples and boundary | Default execution policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A0 — Isolated preparation | Draft text, calculation or synthetic sandbox artifact; no protected-data disclosure or external persistent effects. | Within scoped task authority, autonomous execution permitted with resource limits. |
| A1 — Scoped read | Authorized retrieval within permitted user/tenant/purpose; no new external disclosure. | Bounded preauthorization allowed; enforce resource/data restrictions and audit metadata. |
| A2 — Bounded reversible internal write | Update an internal draft or allowlisted nonproduction record with verified rollback and no consequential downstream trigger. | Explicit bounded preauthorization allowed; object/field/count/time limits and recovery required. |
| A3 — External or consequential write | Send/publish content, external sensitive-data transfer, protected-branch merge, production configuration, destructive operations, financial commitment, account suspension or permission change. | Per-action human approval of the exact operation, current authorization and limits; batch approval only for an immutable enumerated batch. |
| A4 — Safety/rights-sensitive decision or action | Clinical instruction, eligibility/employment/lending decision, physical actuation or other material effects on persons. | Default deny autonomous execution; requires a separately accepted sector/use-case profile and qualified accountable human review before dispatch. |
| AX — Unknown or prohibited | Unclassified tool/effect, unsupported legal purpose, insufficient authority, required approval unavailable, or forbidden data destination. | Deny execution; request scope/policy clarification through the authorized channel. |

Financial actions are A3 unless an explicitly reviewed narrower module defines permissible bounded automation. This initial profile grants no generic spending allowance. Account credential, permission or approval-policy changes cannot be A2. A2 preauthorization must be deliberate; task intent alone does not establish an unlimited write envelope.

### B2. Preauthorization and approval record

Preauthorization binds authenticated principal, tenant, agent/workload, approved tool/version, resource selector, allowed operations/fields, purpose, destination, maximum count/cost, time window and revocation mechanism. Do not invent monetary or duration defaults: owners set and approve concrete limits before enabling the tool. Missing mandatory limits means deny. Disallow wildcard grants where they exceed the documented task need.

Per-action approval includes: unique approval ID, approver identity/authority, originating principal, tenant, tool/schema/policy versions, operation and resource identifiers, canonical parameters or their integrity binding, destination, amount/currency when applicable, expected side effects, maximum scope, expiration, one-time/batch usage rule and revocation state. The preview must show decision-relevant data from the execution record, not a model-authored summary alone. Sensitive values may be protected in storage but must remain integrity-bound and visible to the authorized approver where necessary.

Canonicalization must be deterministic and versioned; test Unicode, number formatting, field order and equivalent resource identifiers. Resolve destinations and mutable resource versions at approval and dispatch. Material changes require reapproval. A harmless reordering of identically bound fields must not alter the action; an omitted or changed consequential field must. Tokens must be unforgeable and approvals consumed atomically to prevent concurrent replay. Approver authority must be checked again at dispatch; revocation or expired delegation blocks execution.

### B3. Execution sequence and failure behavior

1. Resolve requested effect and classify all applicable action classes.
2. Validate typed parameters and approved tool/schema; reject unknown or prohibited capability changes.
3. Enforce current user/agent/tenant/resource authority outside the model, including data destination and purpose.
4. Validate bounded preauthorization or obtain required exact-action approval. Approval cannot waive missing authority.
5. Immediately before dispatch, recheck policy, resource version, approval binding/expiry/revocation and remaining limits; atomically reserve limits and consume approval.
6. Dispatch with least-privilege credentials and a stable operation/idempotency key where supported; log redacted decision metadata and provider receipt.
7. Record completed, failed-before-dispatch, or uncertain-external-state. For uncertain completion, stop blind retries and reconcile against provider state.
8. On suspension, prevent new dispatch; attempt supported cancellation; identify actions already accepted and recovery/notification ownership.

Policy, authorization, approval or budget-service outages fail closed for consequential writes. A dependency outage may allow only previously specified harmless preparation; it cannot silently downgrade A3 to A2. Approval does not make an unsafe or invalid operation valid. Agents cannot approve themselves, rewrite their own budgets or alter enforcement policy. Detect lower-impact actions that compose into a higher-impact chain; enforce cumulative limits across sessions and related tasks where the policy specifies them.

### B4. Required policy fixtures

| ID | Attempt | Expected result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P01 | Internal authorized read under exact scoped grant. | Permit A1; log actor/tenant/policy and outcome. |
| P02 | Same read sends confidential results to an unapproved external endpoint. | Reclassify A3 or AX; deny absent valid destination permission and exact approval. |
| P03 | Approved $100 transfer changed to $101, another currency or another recipient. | Reject binding; reapproval required. |
| P04 | Two concurrent requests replay the same single-use approval. | At most one dispatch; other request denied; uncertain completion reconciled without blind retry. |
| P05 | Approver loses authority after approval but before dispatch. | Deny. |
| P06 | A2 write triggers customer email or production change. | Reclassify A3; scoped A2 grant insufficient. |
| P07 | Agent splits a large action into many below-limit operations. | Cumulative policy limit holds; deny excess; no session-reset bypass. |
| P08 | Tool endpoint/schema changes after approval. | Deny until trusted capability review and required reapproval. |
| P09 | Provider times out after receiving a financial request. | Mark uncertain; reconcile by operation key/provider state; no duplicate blind dispatch. |
| P10 | Kill switch invoked with pending actions. | No new dispatch after acknowledged suspension; report accepted external actions and supported cancellation results. |
| P11 | Eligibility decision without the accepted sector profile. | A4 autonomous execution denied; human escalation does not by itself supply a valid profile. |
| P12 | Policy/approval service unavailable. | Consequential write denied; reason recorded without secrets. |
