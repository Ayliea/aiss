# RFC 0001 — AISS v1.2 Initial Public Ratification

## Summary

This RFC retroactively documents the initial public release of the Ayliea AI Security Standard (AISS) at version 1.2 on 2026-05-11. It establishes the baseline scope, structure, and governance for the standard: 10 control domains, 56 sub-controls, 87 audit questions, a 5-level maturity model, and 9 framework crosswalks. All subsequent RFCs build on the structure ratified here.

## Motivation

AISS exists because the AI security control landscape is fragmented across overlapping frameworks — NIST AI RMF, NIST AI 600-1, ISO/IEC 42001, OWASP LLM Top 10, MITRE ATLAS, EU AI Act, Colorado AI Act, and several others — none of which on its own gives a practitioner a single auditable posture assessment. AISS unifies the practitioner-relevant subset of these sources into a coherent, scoreable, version-controlled standard, published under CC-BY-4.0 so any organization can fork or adapt it.

Earlier internal drafts (v1.0 and v1.1) lived only in the Ayliea platform's code. v1.2 is the first version published as an open standard with a normative spec, dedicated documentation, and a public governance process.

## Detailed design

### Scope (v1.2)

- **10 control domains**, identified `AC-1` through `AC-10`:
  - `AC-1` Governance and Policy
  - `AC-2` Asset Management
  - `AC-3` Data Protection
  - `AC-4` Access Control
  - `AC-5` Supply Chain Security
  - `AC-6` Output Validation
  - `AC-7` Incident Response
  - `AC-8` Monitoring and Logging
  - `AC-9` Training and Awareness
  - `AC-10` Model Security
- **56 sub-controls** across the 10 domains.
- **87 assessment questions** in the Ayliea platform's reference implementation, each citing its sub-control by `standardRef`.
- **5-level maturity model**: 0 (None) / 3 (Initial) / 5 (Defined) / 8 (Managed) / 10 (Optimized), with `maxPoints = 10` per question for direct comparability across controls.
- **9 framework crosswalks** in `crosswalks/`: NIST CSF 2.0, NIST AI RMF, NIST AI 600-1, CIS Controls v8.1, ISO/IEC 27001:2022, MITRE ATLAS v5.6, OWASP LLM Top 10 (2025 edition), EU AI Act, Colorado AI Act.
- **Machine-readable spec** at `spec/aiss-v1.2.json` covering every domain, sub-control, scoring rule, and crosswalk in a stable schema for downstream tools.

### Authoritative sources

Every sub-control cites one or more external authorities. Sources were read from their normative text, not from summaries or training material:

- NIST AI RMF 1.0 (2023-01) and the Generative AI Profile NIST AI 600-1 (2024-07)
- ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (Annex A)
- CIS Controls v8.1 (2024-06)
- OWASP LLM Top 10 2025 (published 2025-03)
- MITRE ATLAS v5.6 (from authoritative `atlas-data` repository)
- EU AI Act Regulation 2024/1689 (Article 50 effective 2026-08-02)
- Colorado AI Act SB24-205

## Drawbacks and trade-offs

- **Yet-another-framework risk.** AISS adds to the field of overlapping standards rather than reducing it. Mitigated by: AISS does not duplicate any of its sources, it stitches them into a single auditable posture — the value is in the integration, not the content.
- **Scope concentration on operational controls.** AISS v1.2 emphasizes runtime AI risk (data protection, output validation, model security, monitoring) and is deliberately lighter on lifecycle / governance maturity than ISO/IEC 42001. Practitioners needing deep AI management-system governance should pair AISS with 42001 — the crosswalk is provided for this case.
- **Solo maintainer.** Until a community forms around the standard, change velocity and review depth depend on one person. Mitigated by: open RFC process, public discussion, CC-BY-4.0 fork-ability.

## Alternatives considered

- **Adopt an existing framework instead.** Considered NIST AI RMF (too high-level for direct scoring), ISO/IEC 42001 (paywalled, audit-heavy, lifecycle-focused not control-focused), and OWASP LLM Top 10 (threat-list, not a posture standard). None gave a single scoreable assessment surface. AISS bridges them.
- **Keep the framework proprietary.** Rejected. Proprietary AI security frameworks already exist (vendor scoring rubrics from Vanta, Drata, etc.) and are structurally untrustworthy as audit artifacts. The Glass-Box positioning requires the standard itself to be public.
- **Publish only the spec, not the methodology + crosswalks.** Rejected. A spec without the reasoning behind it is not auditable; auditors and forks need the rationale to make judgment calls in edge cases.

## Adoption and migration

This RFC documents an already-released version. There is no migration; v1.2 is the public baseline.

The Ayliea platform consumes AISS as its primary AI Security framework. Assessments taken before v1.2 was published reference v1.0 / v1.1 internally; their question text is preserved via `question_published_versions` snapshots so their scoring remains reproducible against the version they were taken under.

## Unresolved questions

Future RFCs may address:

- **AI Agent Security** — whether to fold into AISS via a new sub-control series or maintain it as a separate Tier-2 framework
- **Supply chain extension** — formal AylieaLabs methodology document for AI/MCP/agent third-party risk (separate from any in-product vendor module)
- **Methodology refinements** — possible weighting adjustments, additional maturity bands, or partial-credit semantics
- **Insurance carrier crosswalks** — mapping AISS to cyber-insurance underwriting questionnaires
- **Vertical bundles** — Healthcare, FinServ, EdTech overlays as separate sub-standards or as crosswalk overlays
- **AIVSS** — once published (~end 2026), evaluate as either a crosswalk or a vulnerability-scoring overlay
